02 / TWO BARRIERS

Two walls can be clearer than one.

Two barriers of height V = 1 and width 1, four apart. At a few energies the wave runs back and forth between them in step and the pair lets the whole wave through; between those energies the pair is darker than either wall alone. Choose an energy and send a packet, or put a wave between the walls and time its escape.

03 / TWO SLITS

One wave, two openings, stripes.

The same Schrödinger equation in two dimensions. A packet of wavelength λ meets a wall with two slits of width a, d apart. Each slit sends out its own wave; where the two arrive in step the screen is bright, where they arrive half a wavelength apart it is dark. Close one slit, or watch which slit the wave took, and the stripes go.

FIELD NOTES

Below the top, still through.

The packet's mean energy (0.50) is below a barrier of 0.6, yet about a third of it comes out on the far side. Raise the barrier and the transmitted share falls quickly; lower it and most of the packet passes.

The same solver as the terminal game

Crank–Nicolson on 3,900 points (Δx = 0.1, Δt = 0.05), solved in C and compiled to WebAssembly. JavaScript only draws and reads inputs. The painting and Hop are illustrations: Hop rests on the highest point of the density, which is not a measured particle position.
